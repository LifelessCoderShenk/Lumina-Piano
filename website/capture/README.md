### Revised hand export

Wrist reference study: SeeMusic's homepage was inspected visually and describes synchronized player footage (https://www.seemusicapp.com/). Schott Music's circular/lateral wrist lesson (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yvYXgUANfEQ) was found via search, but the embedded player returned error 153; it was not watched. Lumina now rotates finger roots and relaxed tips with the wrist, and uses a small lift/turn/settle arc during lateral travel. These are original procedural approximations, not extracted motion capture.

The finger rig uses three articulated segments with fixed length ratios, solving joint bends in a hand-specific plane. Reachable poses preserve segment lengths through rest, press and release. Unreachable assignments now stop at the joint/reach limit and report a reach error instead of proportionally stretching bones. The planner is not a complete collision-constrained solver.

Automatic fingering also scores sustained notes across onsets: occupied-finger reuse, crossed held fingers and excessive spans carry penalties. Manual fingering remains authoritative. These are planning preferences, not guarantees that arbitrary MIDI is physically playable.

render-hands.html re-exports the original miniature through the actual ExportEngine after the hand anatomy and motion changes. Both the standalone performance and the result portion of the walkthrough use this revised export. The walkthrough UI stills remain the original captures. Fingers now have clearer separation, a smaller palm, eased transitions and a curved lift between notes; nearby notes share a steadier wrist position. Idle fingers use a tucked resting pose with subtle neighbor movement. Wrist targets include sustained notes, and releases ease gently toward rest. Thumbs bend toward the side of each palm. This remains procedural MIDI-inferred motion, not recorded human fingering.


### Skeletal 3D implementation (October 22)

The flat silhouette shader has been replaced with an original procedural skinned mesh: a wrist, forearm, and five hierarchical four-joint chains (22 bones). Three.js performs skin deformation; a shared depth prepass prevents overlapping transparent surfaces from accumulating. Wrist yaw, flexion and roll drive the palm; the forearm follows toward the player, while reachable fingertips remain on their MIDI contacts. Thumb bend is more limited than the long fingers. No external meshes or motion recordings were imported.

Create mode reserves 12% of frame height below the keyboard when hands are enabled. Keyboard dimensions are unchanged. The layout snapshot includes this space in offline export. The video remains an original 23.8-second MIDI performance at 1080p/30fps. The edited walkthrough still uses its original UI stills followed by the revised export.

Research references: [FürElise](https://for-elise.github.io/) combines motion references with MIDI contact refinement; its [dataset](https://huggingface.co/datasets/rcwang/for_elise) is CC BY-NC 4.0 and was not imported. [Natural piano hand motion](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/tvrsj/15/3/15_KJ00007408731/_article/-char/ja/) supports combining rest poses, anticipation and inverse kinematics. The implementation uses [Three.js skeletal skinning](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/SkinnedMesh.html), not either research system's learned model. Current limitations: stylized procedural anatomy, inferred fingering, no captured pianist timing, and no complete hand-to-hand or finger-to-finger collision solver. Do not describe the result as photorealistic or equivalent to captured human motion.
